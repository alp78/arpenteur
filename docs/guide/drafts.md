# Drafts

One proposed message per company, written by a model from what is held about the company and from your message brief, for a person to read. A draft is never sent by itself: approving records that a person read it, and sending happens from a campaign wave or from your own mail client.

## The list

The counts at the top say how many drafts await review, how many are approved and not sent, sent, refused, and how many versions of the brief they were written against. Each row shows the language, the company, the subject, the address and the age, with a button to read it. The list can be narrowed to one language.

Two banners can appear above the list:

- some drafts carry a missing-value marker, because a field under Settings, Enrolment was empty when they were written; they cannot be approved until the setting is filled and the drafts are written again
- some unsent drafts were written against an older version of the brief

## The draft's page

The page shows the company, the address, the language, the state, the digest of the brief and the model, then the subject and the body. Wording the brief forbids is marked.

- Approve records that you read it. Nothing is sent.
- Mark as sent, available once approved, records that you sent it from your own mail client, and moves the company's card to "contacted" on the programme named under Settings, Enrolment, inserting it when it was not a member.
- Undo approval takes an approved draft back to review.
- Refuse, with a reason for the next run, closes the draft; a later run will not propose this mailbox again.

## Where the drafts come from

The Draft the first messages card on Operations writes them:

- it takes companies with an address that was found and checked as deliverable
- it writes in the language of the canton
- it reads the message brief named under Settings, Enrolment, and the invitation from the programme's templates folder when the setting says so
- it refuses without a mailbox, because the opt-out sentence names its address
- the batch is capped at 25 by default, because every draft is read by a person

A company that already has an open draft gets it replaced, and the new one waits for review again.
