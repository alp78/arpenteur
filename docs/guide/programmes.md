# Programmes

One endeavour across many companies, most of which will never be clients: a pilot offered to fifty firms, a partner search, a recruitment of testers. Each member sits at its own stage, and the question every morning is how many replied, how many are testing, and who has gone quiet.

A client has projects; a programme has members. The two are different shapes, and a company can be a member of two programmes at two different stages.

## Your programmes

The table lists each programme with its status, its dates, how many members it has, a tally per stage and what it is. The form under it starts one with a code, a name and a description. A new programme starts with eight stages: selected, contacted, replied, onboarding, testing, live, refused and parked. They can be renamed on the programme's page.

## The programme's board

One column per stage, with the stage's note, and one card per member: the name, the canton and legal form, the contact, how much mail came in and when, how long the card has sat there, and what they said. A member that is also a client carries the client chip.

**Moving a member**

Drag a card to another column, or use the fold "move" under it. Inbound mail is counted on the card and moves nothing: a card that moved because a message arrived would be the first thing to break the rule that nothing in the record files itself.

**Bringing members in**

- The shortlist form takes the best-scoring companies of a sector, a set of cantons and a fit band, up to a number, and adds them. It only ever adds.
- The single form takes a UID or part of a name and a contact, for one company at a time.

**Feedback**

The fold "they said" under a card records a note, a bug, a request or praise, in the firm's words, dated. The panel "what they said" collects them across the programme.

**The stages**

The stages are your words, one per line with an optional note after a colon. Removing a stage that members stand in is refused, and the refusal counts them: absorbing that edit would mean moving members somewhere nobody chose.

## The ledger

The ledger answers "where does each firm stand" in one row per member: the stage, what went out, what came back, when they last wrote, and the enrolment they answered with. The counts above the table say how many answered, how many were written to and stayed silent, how many addresses failed, how many asked to stop, and how many enrolled.

A firm reading "3 out, 0 in, address failed" was never reached, and on the board it looks like a firm that ignored three messages. The ledger tells the two apart.

**Enrolments**

A reply to an invitation names a platform and a tester's account. The Read the replies stage proposes the reading, and the ledger shows a form to record it; nothing is enrolled until a person records it. Agreement to a dated window is asked rather than read, because a reply declining the dates carries the same words as one accepting them. A reply asking to be left alone suppresses the address, which is the one thing that stage writes.

The tester's account lives on the member and nowhere else: an account volunteered in a reply is different in kind from an address taken off a public website.

**A member's history**

Every message to and from one firm, oldest first.

## Where the moves come from

- Marking a draft as sent, on Drafts, moves the firm to "contacted" on the programme named under Settings, Enrolment, inserting it when it was not a member. It moves forward only.
- A campaign wave sent to a programme's list moves its recipients the same way.
- Everything else is moved by hand.
