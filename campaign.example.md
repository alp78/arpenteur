# Scoring brief, an example

This file is an example of what the `match` stage scores companies against. Copy it to `campaign.md`, or write yours under Settings, and replace every section with your own offer: the file is read verbatim into the prompt, so it is the single thing that decides whether a fit score means anything. A brief written for someone else's business produces confident scores built on nothing.

`campaign.md` is yours and stays out of git. Every score stores a digest of the brief it was taken against, so rewriting the brief invalidates the old scores, and the next `match` re-scores exactly what the rewrite made stale.

Write it in the language you will write to companies in; the approach advice comes back in the same one. The example below describes a fictional independent bookkeeper in French-speaking Switzerland.

---

## What we do

An independent bookkeeper and payroll accountant, twelve years in fiduciary practice, working alone from Lausanne for small businesses across Vaud and Fribourg. Three services: monthly bookkeeping and VAT returns, payroll with the social insurance declarations, and the year-end closing with the tax return.

The client talks to the person who keeps the books. No junior staff, no call centre, no software resold on commission. The accounting file belongs to the client and is handed over in a standard format on request.

The first engagement is a review of the current books at a fixed price, before any monthly mandate.

## Who we serve best

Businesses of two to twenty people with no accountant on staff, whose owner signs the invoices and wants the books kept without having to think about them. Trades, shops, restaurants, small workshops, independent practices and associations with paid staff.

Three situations weigh more than the sector:

- A business that has just hired its first employee and must declare payroll for the first time
- A bookkeeper who is retiring, or a fiduciary that raised its prices beyond what a small file justifies
- An owner who still keeps the books in a spreadsheet and files the VAT return late

## Who we serve badly

- Fiduciaries and accounting firms: colleagues and competitors, not clients
- Companies with more than fifty employees, which need an in-house accountant
- Holding companies and investment vehicles, whose questions are legal before they are accounting
- A company in liquidation, in moratorium or under published financial difficulty

## What we look for

- A registered purpose that describes a trade, a shop, a practice or a workshop
- A recent incorporation, a change of owner, or a move to a larger site
- A website that lists staff, which means payroll
- No fiduciary named on the website or in the register notices
- Vaud or Fribourg as the seat

## What warns us off

- A fiduciary, a trust company or an accountant named as the domicile or as an officer
- A purpose that describes financial, legal or accounting services
- A seat outside the two cantons served
- A registry status other than active

## Proof we can point to

- Sixty monthly mandates kept over twelve years, none lost to a filing penalty
- Payroll for businesses of one to thirty employees, including hospitality with its collective agreement
- Year-end closings accepted without correction by the cantonal tax office

## How we open a conversation

One concrete observation drawn from the register or the website, one sentence on a comparable mandate, and one open question about how the books are kept at present. The tone is that of a practitioner writing to an owner, not of a salesperson.

Never a meeting imposed, never a price in a first message, never a promise of savings. At most a first conversation without obligation.

One hundred and fifty words at most. In French, using the formal address.
